# Hong Kong Stock Market Basics

An overview of how the Hong Kong securities market works, focused on facts that
matter when analyzing HK-listed equities.

## Market Structure

The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (SEHK) is operated by Hong Kong Exchanges and
Clearing Limited (HKEX). Main trading hours are 09:30-12:00 and 13:00-16:00 HKT,
with a pre-opening session before 09:30. The market is closed on weekends and
Hong Kong public holidays.

## Key Indices

- Hang Seng Index (HSI): the flagship benchmark of the largest and most liquid
  HK-listed companies. It is a free-float market-cap weighted index.
- Hang Seng Tech Index: covers the largest technology-themed companies listed in
  Hong Kong.
- Hang Seng China Enterprises Index (HSCEI): tracks H-shares, i.e., mainland
  Chinese companies incorporated in China and listed in HK.

## Trading Conventions

- Lot size: unlike US markets, HK stocks trade in board lots set by each issuer,
  commonly 100, 500, 1000 or 2000 shares. Orders smaller than one lot are called
  odd lots and usually trade at a discount.
- Price ticks: minimum price increments vary by price band (e.g., HK$0.01 for
  prices below HK$0.25, larger ticks for higher prices).
- Settlement: T+2. A trade executed on Monday settles on Wednesday. CCASS is the
  central securities depository.
- Trading fees include brokerage commission, SEHK trading fee, SFC transaction
  levy, and stamp duty (currently 0.1% of consideration, rounded up to the nearest
  HK$1, charged on both buy and sell).

## Security Types

- Blue chips: large, liquid constituents such as HSI members.
- H-shares: mainland-incorporated companies listed in HK, denominated in HKD but
  whose underlying business reports in RMB.
- Red chips and P-chips: mainland-affiliated companies incorporated outside
  mainland China.
- Dual primary/secondary listings: many large Chinese technology companies list in
  HK in addition to US listings, and may later convert to dual primary listing to
  become eligible for Stock Connect.

## Stock Connect

Northbound trading allows mainland investors to buy eligible HK/China Connect
securities; southbound trading allows HK and international investors to buy
eligible A-shares through the HK exchange. Stock Connect flows are a widely watched
signal for HK market sentiment.

## Practical Notes for Analysis

- HKD is pegged to USD within a narrow band (about 7.75-7.85), so HKD interest
  rates closely follow USD rates.
- Many HK-listed companies pay dividends semi-annually; dividend yield is an
  important valuation metric in this market.
- Liquidity concentrates in large caps; small-cap turnover can be very thin,
  which amplifies price impact.
